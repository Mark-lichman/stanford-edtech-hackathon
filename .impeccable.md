## Design Context

### Users

Lantern is for children roughly 4–12. Children ages 4–9 commonly use it with a
parent or another trusted adult; children ages 10–12 should be able to use it
independently. The product helps a child move from something they already love
into one small real-world action, then return to a story that advances and ends.
The adult supports the ritual for younger children without turning the product
into a dashboard about the child.

### Brand Personality

Curious, playful, fun. The experience should evoke invitation, wonder, and the
pleasure of making something happen off-screen. It should not feel like school
work, a rewards app, or an engagement-maximizing game.

### Aesthetic Direction

A soft, friendly storybook system with a blue-led palette, rounded sans fonts,
chunky high-contrast components, generous white space, and warm supporting
accents. It should feel like calm adventure: playful enough for children, but
clean and composed enough for preteens. Use progressive disclosure so younger
children receive more scaffolding while older children receive more autonomy.

The system may use rounded page surfaces and choice treatments, but it should
not become a dashboard or a grid of reward cards. Yellow signals celebration;
coral/pink signals character and quest moments. Story text remains at least
20pt, with readable line-height and optional accessibility typography.

### Design Principles

1. One page, one invitation: keep each moment focused on one story beat or one
   tiny action.
2. Screen as doorway: every interaction should point toward a real-world act,
   a shared reading moment, or a natural stopping point.
3. Confidence before complexity: widen choices only when the child demonstrates
   comfort, never because the product needs more engagement.
4. Artifacts over scores: celebrate what the child made or did, not an identity,
   ranking, streak, badge, XP value, or performance number.
5. The ending is success: design the product to become unnecessary and hand the
   child back to books, people, and the world.
