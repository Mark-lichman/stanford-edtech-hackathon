## 2026-11-04 — First outreach wave: 12 prospects, three messages, scored Dec 10

- **12 prospects in wave 1, not all 39.** That's as many calls as three people can take, and
  it tests the copy before using up the list. E2 (kit makers) waits until it has 10 prospects.
- **Three messages, then stop.** Each follow-up adds something new or makes the ask smaller.
  The last one closes the loop with no guilt. Replies roughly halve with each step, and
  being respectful matters more than squeezing out extra replies.
- **E4 asks a group lead for a conversation, not for a share.** Volunteer-run parent groups
  shouldn't be asked to forward strangers' emails to their members.
- **Sent from Mark's Gmail, reviewed before sending.** No open tracking.
- **Gate: 15% replies.** Under 10% means rewrite the first message before wave 2.
  Details and sources are in [outreach/README.md](outreach/README.md).

## 2026-11-04 — Nonprofits are design partners, not funders

Outreach to literacy nonprofits asks for access to children and co-design input, not money.

- The sprint's sponsor research found that every verified case of a third party paying
  so a product reaches families free at home had a nonprofit or public institution in the
  chain (Waterford Upstart, Khan Academy, public libraries).
- The same research found no case of a sponsor underwriting a product that didn't exist
  yet, and nearly every foundation funder requires 501(c)(3) status. Asking for money now
  spends the relationship before there is anything to fund.
- What teams of our size did successfully ran through end users first. A nonprofit that
  runs K-3 reading sessions is the shortest path to real children with a trusted adult
  in the room.

Evidence: Viability Sprint doc, "Sponsors and money" tab.

## 2026-09-30 — Two-week viability sprint (Mark, Amlan, Aditya)

From the sprint plan, recorded here so the log starts at the real beginning:

- Distribution is **B2B2C**: a platform or sponsor carries distribution and cost, and the
  family pays nothing or close to it. No selling to parents and no chasing districts during the sprint.
- **No software and no recording a child.** One hand-built chapter for one real child,
  parent present, handwritten notes only.
- Owners: Amlan for sponsor outreach, Mark for the product and rule set, Aditya for the
  child's side (consent, field time, parent and tutor interviews).
- The rule set (what keeps a generated chapter engaging, safe, decodable, and its quest
  achievable alone and indoors) is the product.
- Kids' activity-kit companies were added to the target list because they already solve
  achievable, age-graded activities, which is Lantern's quest problem.
